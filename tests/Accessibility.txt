In accordance with Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, our
information must be comparably accessible and usable between disabled
and able-bodied members of the public.
This list will also take inspiration from the Web Content Accessibility
Guidelines (WCAG) 2 requirements, despite our project not being an official
government project.
Reference website for 508 Guidelines:
https://www.ada.gov/resources/small-entity-compliance-guide/#1-archived-web-content
Reference website for WCAG 2:
https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/
Upon further discussion(9/22/26) it has been determined that certain features should
not be implemented in this semester's build of OpenPath RPI. Therefore, any parts of
the checklist that are no longer necessary/adjusted for our current build will from
this point on be labelled with an asterisk(*) after the rule number, indicating them
as optional. They will be bottom priority items until all previous accessibility goals
and website functions are realized. They will be expanded upon below in the section
titled "Optional Items".
---------------------Ease of information access----------------------------------------
1. Check that a screen reader can read the route directions.
2. Ensure that the Google read-aloud functions properly on our website.
3.* A verbal transcription option of the optimal route determined by our program.
4. Visuals should use text cues alongside color cues in a consistent, non-conditional
   manner.
5. Proper alternative text should be available for all non-text content. This text
   should effectively convey all necessary information that the content would
   otherwise provide to the user.
6. Ensure proper color contrast ratio. A minimum of 4.5:1 contrast between text color
   and its background is acceptable. For large text (18 point/14 point bold or higher
   on client end), this minimum is lowered to 3:1. For graphical objects and user-
   interface components (e.g. buttons, text boxes), the contrast between these objects
   and their surroundings also requires a minimum of 3:1. For clarity, user-interface
   components can accomplish this through having their border meet the contrast
   requirements even if the non-text based inner parts (e.g. button color) do not meet
   these requirements.
7. Our website should also be able to scale reasonably with page zoom and text-size
   adjustment features for computer and mobile devices respectively.
8. Check that the site remains usable up to and including 200% page zoom.
9. All user input must be given a title describing their purpose.
10. All site functions must be accessible with keyboard alone. For example, the
   specification of disabilities/unusable traversal methods must be achievable without
   a mouse.
11. If needed/possible, employ a programmatically determined website structure. That
   is, any information that needs to be given in a specific order must be processed by
   screen readers in that order. This can be accomplished through markup language and
   Accessible Rich Internet Applications(ARIA). Only use ARIA if the behavior cannot
   be performed in base HTML.
12. Ensure that all instructions to press a certain button offer means of
   identifying said button without any knowledge of its shape, size, or relative
   position. Avoid terms like "the green button on the right", try "the button
   that says 'go'".
13. Avoid flashing items on-screen (potential epileptic hazard).
14. If CAPTCHA is incorporated, provide modes for different disabilities.
15. If an error is detected, it should be identified and described to the user in
   text.
16. Ensure all statements in this list apply to all screen sizes and orientations
   (e.g. computer, phone, tablet, landscape, portrait).
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Proper documentation will be required on how we account for disabilities.

   our program.
